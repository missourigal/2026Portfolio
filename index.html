How I work

Three things that show up in every project

01

AI-augmented, not AI-outsourced

I use agentic workflows to generate and stress-test directions at a scale that changes what's feasible — then bring the judgement the agent can't: what to build and what to refuse, what looks wrong, which question to ask, and when the work is finished rather than merely polished.

02

Systems that outlast the project

Tokens, documentation and component libraries built so engineers, designers, PMs and AI agents all work from one source of truth. The system is usually the reason the second half of a project moves faster than the first.

03

Designs engineers can actually build

Research and strategy through IA, interaction and visual design — then staying with it through implementation. Having shipped production front-end myself, I design inside real constraints, anticipate where a pattern will fight the stack, and write the spec that keeps the intent intact once someone else is building it.

About

Fifteen years of making complex software feel simple

I'm a senior product designer with 15+ years in B2B SaaS, across large organisations and early-stage startups — equally comfortable owning product strategy, shaping design systems, and crafting pixel-perfect UI.

Lately that includes AI-driven agentic workflows, used to rapidly generate and stress-test design directions before they ever reach a whiteboard or a Figma file. As Director of UX at ACT! (Swiftpage), I lead design across a CRM platform used by thousands of sales and customer-facing teams — from IA and interaction patterns to a design system rebuilt with tokens, markdown documentation and AI-ready skill files usable by developers, designers, PMs and AI agents alike.

The work before that taught me different things. At GE Aviation I designed for commercial airline pilots, where the constraint was a dark cockpit, ninety minutes, and users with every reason to distrust a new tool. At Cox Automotive I worked at enterprise scale, consolidating competing design systems across a family of brands. And for nearly a decade at BountyJobs I was the sole designer on a two-sided marketplace, owning IA, interaction, visual design and front-end implementation — a first-hand education in what it actually takes to ship as a design team of one.

For nearly a decade I implemented my own designs in production — HTML, CSS and JavaScript — and although I'm not in the codebase today, that's the part of my background engineers notice first. It means I can scope what's feasible before it costs a sprint, understand why one pattern is cheap and another expensive, and write specs that survive contact with implementation. When I need something clickable now, I build it with AI-assisted tooling rather than in a code editor.

I care about software that's approachable for beginners and powerful for experts, and about creating leverage that goes beyond a traditional engineering + product + design structure.
