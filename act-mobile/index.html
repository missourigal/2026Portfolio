01 · Starting with why

Diagnose it first, then decide what to build

ACT!'s existing mobile app was underperforming, but rather than assume we knew why, I ran a research shareout to diagnose the actual causes.

That research became the case I brought to leadership: the right move wasn't another round of patches on a weak foundation. It was a rebuild. Getting that decision right mattered more than any single screen that followed — a rebuild is expensive to reverse, so the research had to hold up.

I interviewed 5 existing mobile users to ground the rebuild in real behaviour, then validated direction through wireframes and a clickable prototype before committing to build. Two decisions came directly out of that testing: bottom navigation over a side slide-out menu, once testing showed it was faster for one-handed, on-the-go use; and pulling proximity search — nearby-contact lookup — into the MVP itself, after testing showed it was the single feature most likely to drive adoption.

Owning it end to end

Research, information architecture, interaction design and visual design — then working alongside engineering through implementation.

Contact lists and detail views, filtering and proximity search, calendar and opportunities, activities, notes, and the secondary-contacts work now underway. No hand-off gaps, and no version of the product that existed only as a spec.

03 · The system

A system, not a one-off

Rather than design the app as an isolated project, I built a token-based design system alongside it, documented down to the CSS custom property.

Every colour, spacing value and type size traces back to a primitive token, mapped through a semantic layer into what engineers actually reference in code.

Primitive --color-ocean-blue

#00558F — one named value in the brand palette, with no meaning attached yet.

Semantic --ion-color-primary

Mapped to a purpose — CTAs, active states, links — so intent survives a future palette change.

Component ion-button

What engineers reference directly, on a component library built on Ionic.

The same discipline runs through spacing (a 4px grid, from --space-0 to --space-9), type scale, radii and elevation — plus parallel token sets for light and dark mode. That structure means the system doesn't just describe how the app looked at launch; it's the foundation product and engineering keep building on as the app grows.

The system lives as a fully documented Figma library alongside the code tokens — components, states and usage guidance that designers, engineers and AI tools can all reference the same way.

04 · Why it matters

The system pays off twice

A documented, token-based system speeds up development whether a human or an AI agent is doing the building.

Engineers get one source of truth instead of guessing at a colour or spacing value. Our AI-driven BMAD workflow gets the same structure to generate and stress-test concepts against, so what it produces already matches the system instead of needing to be reconciled with it afterwards.

In practice that means less rework in both directions: fewer "which version is correct" conversations with engineering, and less cleanup after an AI-generated first pass. The system is the reason both loops move faster, not just the AI workflow on its own.

05 · After launch

What changed for users

Reviews consistently cite fast performance, ease of use and synchronised contacts between the cloud app and phone — with proximity search and on-the-go contact retrieval called out as standouts.

But the deeper pattern is what happens after launch. The app has a built-in feedback mechanism, and it directly drives the roadmap. Proximity search filtering and a business-card scanner both shipped this way. None of it was on the original launch plan; users asked, and we built.

"Excellent program. Works like one expects with today's technology. Been using ACT since 1998 and dreaming of a way to have synchronized contacts on my cell... This one does it."

App Store review

That appetite for a fully synced experience pointed to the next step: importing contacts directly from a phone's native address book into ACT!, instead of manual re-entry.

Users reorder, remove or add fields on their own contact form, drag-and-drop, right from the app — built after user testing surfaced how differently teams actually work.
